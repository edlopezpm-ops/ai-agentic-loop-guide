# Context Prompt for Codex

You are helping me prepare for a Salesforce Solution Engineer interview.

## Context

I am Ed Lopez, a former Manhattan Associates Principal Support Consultant / Support Manager / Project Manager with 13+ years of enterprise software experience. My core background is in supply chain software, especially Manhattan SCALE WMS, SQL Server, integrations, APIs, troubleshooting, implementations, technical consulting, customer support escalation, and post-go-live stabilization.

I am interviewing for a Salesforce Solution Engineer / Pre-Sales role. The hiring manager is Puja Naik, Senior Manager of Solution Engineering at Salesforce and manager of Fabio, a former colleague who reported to me for a period of time and highly recommended me internally. Fabio told me Puja is very bright, strong on business/value/leadership, but not deeply technical.

## Role Context

This is not a pure developer role. It is a pre-sales Solution Engineering role. The SE works with Account Executives, customers, technical teams, and internal Salesforce stakeholders to:

- understand customer business problems quickly
- perform discovery and requirements gathering
- translate business needs into technical solution options
- validate feasibility with technical/product teams
- prepare short decks or demos quickly, sometimes within 1 hour
- tell a clear solution story to the customer
- build confidence that Salesforce understands the problem and can solve it
- support new customers, expansions, new modules, and existing customer growth
- bridge sales pressure, technical feasibility, and customer expectations

## Important Insight

Fabio said internal stakeholders can be complex, especially vendors / Account Executives. Some have strong egos, some sell a lot, some sell little, and the SE must be able to partner with them without killing the sale while still keeping the solution realistic.

## My Positioning

Do NOT position me as "a WMS guy trying to learn Salesforce."

Position me as:

"An enterprise solution consultant with deep technical expertise who has spent his career translating business problems into technically feasible solutions."

## Core Message

"Customers rarely buy technology. They buy confidence that their business problem has been understood, that the proposed solution is realistic, and that the team in front of them can actually deliver it."

## Key Themes

- discovery before solutioning
- business problem solving
- translating ambiguous customer pain into technical requirements
- executive communication
- technical feasibility
- customer trust
- cross-functional alignment
- storytelling
- fast synthesis under pressure
- consultative selling
- bridging business, sales, and engineering
- handling difficult stakeholders
- explaining technical concepts to non-technical audiences

## Relevant Experience

- 8 years at Manhattan Associates
- 5 years at Ipsos Interactive Services before that
- roles included Senior Consultant, Support Manager, Project Manager, Principal Support Consultant
- led enterprise customer conversations, escalations, technical investigations, implementations, workshops, and post-go-live support
- worked with executives, warehouse operations leaders, IT teams, developers, DBAs, infrastructure, automation vendors, and support teams
- strong in SQL Server, APIs, XML/JSON, REST, C#/.NET context, Java context, Azure, integrations, troubleshooting, and enterprise architecture
- direct customer-facing experience with complex accounts
- used to high-pressure situations where customers need clarity fast

## Interview Preparation Tasks

1. Create a 45-second personal pitch for Puja.
2. Create a 2-minute version of my pitch.
3. Create STAR stories for ambiguous customer problem, difficult stakeholder, translating business requirements into technical solution, recovering customer trust, bridging sales/business expectations with technical feasibility, and presenting a complex technical issue to executives.
4. Create likely questions Puja may ask and strong answers in my voice.
5. Create questions I should ask Puja about the role, team, expectations, ramp-up, Salesforce enablement, Account Executive partnership, and success metrics.
6. Create a 3-slide interview presentation concept where I sell myself as "the solution."
7. Keep everything concise, sharp, executive-friendly, and natural.
8. Tone: professional, confident, warm, not desperate, not overly formal, slightly conversational.
9. Avoid sounding like a generic candidate.
10. Avoid over-indexing on WMS, SQL, or Manhattan-specific jargon unless used as an example.

## Preferred Language

Write in English for interview answers and presentation content. Spanish notes are acceptable only when explaining strategy to Ed.
